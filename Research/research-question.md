# Research Question

## Primary working research question

> How does experimentally imposed controller-to-weapon interaction latency interact with independently manipulated target-motion predictability to affect acquisition time, first-shot accuracy, and corrective aiming behavior during immersive competitive target acquisition?

## Definitions of the main constructs

**Experimentally imposed controller-to-weapon interaction latency** is system-induced delay between a participant’s physical controller action and the corresponding visible response of the virtual weapon or aiming representation. It is conceptually distinct from network round-trip latency, refresh rate, and an expected delay between firing and affecting a target.

**Target-motion predictability** is the extent to which a target’s future motion can be anticipated from available information. “Independently manipulated” means that predictability is treated as a distinct experimental construct rather than inferred solely from speed, path complexity, turn frequency, or other kinematic differences.

**Acquisition time** is the temporal cost of acquiring the target during a target-acquisition episode. It provides a conventional performance measure that can be compared alongside first-shot accuracy and corrective aiming behavior. Its exact start and end events will be operationalized in the experimental design.

**First-shot accuracy** is performance at the first discrete firing attempt in a target-acquisition episode. It represents the outcome of the initial acquisition and shot decision before additional shots or later correction can compensate for an error.

**Corrective aiming behavior** is the online adjustment of aim after movement begins as the participant responds to target-relative error and updated visual information. It concerns the correction process, not merely the final endpoint or total trial duration.

**Immersive competitive target acquisition** is rapid target-directed aiming and firing in an HMD-based task with a competitive/FPS-like performance demand. The term identifies the interaction context; it does not imply that the study reproduces an entire commercial game or live esports match.

## Why the question matters

Prior research shows that latency affects target-directed performance, that moving-target dynamics can change latency costs, that predictability matters for adaptation to delayed visual feedback, and that controlled latency has consequences in both immersive VR and FPS aiming. Those findings come from different task families and do not by themselves show how the factors combine during embodied, rapid shot acquisition.

Examining acquisition time, first-shot accuracy, and corrective aiming behavior separately is important because prediction and feedback may contribute differently across the course of an aiming action. A single aggregate outcome could obscure whether a performance cost appeared in overall acquisition speed, the initial shot decision, online correction, or across multiple processes.

## Scope

The question is limited to the relationship among:

- system-induced controller/weapon interaction latency;
- independently manipulated target-motion predictability;
- immersive HMD competitive target acquisition;
- acquisition time;
- first-shot accuracy; and
- online corrective aiming behavior.

It asks about interaction and behavioral consequences within this defined context. It does not presuppose the direction or size of any effect.

## Explicitly outside the scope

The current question does not claim or attempt to establish:

- that latency with moving targets is unexplored;
- that FPS latency is unexplored;
- that latency in immersive VR target selection is unexplored;
- that latency-by-predictability relationships have never been investigated;
- that this is the first-ever study combining any two of these concepts;
- a universal human latency threshold;
- network-latency effects as equivalent to local controller/weapon latency;
- the effectiveness of latency-compensation or aim-assistance systems;
- long-term adaptation, training transfer, presence, cybersickness, or subjective experience; or
- results, hypotheses, or experimental procedures not yet established.
